# Results and validation details

[Project overview](../README.md) · [Research report](../reports/Research_Report.pdf)

This note separates the current method from the implementations that generated the archived figures. All experiments are numerical simulations.

## Two implementation stages

**Current direct-shadow learning.** Raw setting–outcome histograms are contracted with dual-frame weights of known observables. The learner constructs neither an estimated density matrix nor a complete estimated state-coefficient vector, and does not use positive-semidefinite (PSD) projection. Discovery uses a bounded raw witness with score bound B = 64. Separate independent records support discovery, score refitting, fixed-candidate selection and affine calibration. Known operators may be represented in symmetry-adapted blocks; this does not reconstruct the unknown state.

**Earlier state-model implementation.** The larger benchmark campaigns and the two result figures in this repository were generated by an earlier backend that used a state estimate and PSD stabilization during discovery. Their numerical results are preserved with that provenance. Removing reconstruction has been checked separately, rather than assumed to reproduce every historical run.

## 1. Matched comparisons and reuse of unchanged records

- **Instances:** six preparations at N = 12, five seeds per preparation, 30 instances in total.
- **Preparations:** noisy OAT and Dicke, rotated/dephased OAT, twisted/dephased Dicke, and two OAT–Dicke mixtures.
- **Encoding:** collective Jx, working point x₀ = 0.
- **Calibration:** four independent records of 10⁸ outcomes each, per instance. This large budget studies readout quality under accurate calibration.
- **Learner access:** known symmetry, encoding and instrument models; no preparation recipes, exact states, QFI or historical control banks as learning inputs.
- **Implementation:** earlier state-model backend.

| Search starts per depth | One layer | At most two layers |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| 32 | 69.91% | 76.93% |
| 128 | 72.87% | 78.31% |

Entries are mean CFI/QFI. The four records for each instance have identical hashes across search budgets. Two-layer performance improves in 21 instances, decreases in six and is unchanged in three. More starts improve the mean, without a monotonic guarantee for each search.

At 128 starts, the comparison gives 53.94% for optimized rotations, 64.77% for fixed-π/2 twists with optimized surrounding rotations, 72.87% for one-layer echoes and 78.3142% for echoes with at most two layers. These are attained efficiencies under the searches used, not certified global optima.

The ideal direct-response reference uses the same two-layer control family and search policy, receives exact probabilities, and estimates derivatives by finite differences. It reaches 78.3058%. This comparison checks readout quality against an exact-probability interface; it does not establish a finite-shot resource advantage. At M = 10⁴ and offsets ±0.004, ±0.013, the ratio of shadow/direct-response MSEs averaged over the window ranges from 0.9856 to 1.0175 across the six preparation conditions.

## 2. Broader benchmark and phase distinguishability

The historical benchmark contains 1,500 calibrations: GHZ, OAT and Dicke; 25 conditions per family; 20 seeds per condition. Nine conditions vary N from 4 to 12 at ε = 0.15. Sixteen vary ε from 0 to 0.30 in steps of 0.02 at N = 12. Some searches use controls from earlier optimizations as starting points.

Mean CFI/QFI is 93.66%, compared with 57.82% for the specified fixed readouts: GHZ parity and specified OAT/Dicke quadratures. This broader comparison has different references and initialization from the 30-instance study; the two means should not be combined as if they came from one experiment.

The upper panels of [the readout figure](../assets/readout-evidence.png) show a single Dicke case at N = 12, ε = 0.15 and seed 00. CFI/QFI changes from 31.1% for the fixed readout to 93.0% for the learned echo. The probe QFI remains 42.0907. The count distributions are evaluated at x₀ ± 0.015; increased local Hellinger curvature visualizes greater phase distinguishability. The lower panel belongs to the separate six-preparation study above.

## 3. Frozen-estimator sensing

The [MSE figure](../assets/sensing-error.png) uses N = 8, ε = 0.15 and 20 independent calibrations per family from the earlier state-model campaign. Generators and working points are (Jz, π/2N) for GHZ, and (Jx, 0) for OAT and Dicke. Fresh sensing outcomes are generated after the readout and estimator have been frozen.

The upper panels cover M = 10² to 10⁸ at the working point. Each displayed M summarizes 400 batch MSEs per family; whiskers span the full observed range. The lower panels pool 102,400 individual errors per family at M = 10⁴.

| Probe | Mean normalized squared error, M FQ × MSE |
| :--- | ---: |
| GHZ | 1.005 |
| OAT | 1.102 |
| Dicke | 1.058 |

For a fixed estimator, MSE is the sum of a variance term proportional to 1/M and a squared bias term. Finite calibration can therefore produce a floor. The QFI reference describes ideal local mean-error performance; individual errors may lie on either side of it.

## 4. Paired validation of current direct-shadow learning

The completed paired archive contains 18 instances: GHZ, OAT and Dicke; N = 4 and 8; ε = 0.15; three seeds per condition. Both implementations use the same calibration records and echo architecture.

| Metric | Result |
| :--- | ---: |
| Earlier state-model mean CFI/QFI | 96.9031% |
| Current direct-shadow mean CFI/QFI | 96.9680% |
| Mean of instance-level MSE ratios, direct/earlier | 1.00742501 |
| Range of instance-level MSE ratios | 0.99036525–1.09189882 |

For each instance, MSE is first averaged equally over offsets −0.015, −0.005, 0, 0.005 and 0.015 at M = 10,000. Ratios are then computed and averaged across instances; the reported number is not the ratio of two pooled MSE means.

These comparisons also change finite-sample stabilization, from PSD-based optimization to bounded raw witnesses. They demonstrate similar average performance on the tested conditions, not bitwise equality or a universal equivalence theorem. Learning-stage access audits for all 18 instances recorded no estimated density matrix, complete estimated state coefficients, PSD projection or exact-state/QFI access.

Separate operator-query checks at N = 4, 8 and 12 agree with raw linear PI inversion to floating-point precision: maximum absolute differences are 1.6682 × 10⁻¹⁴ for responses and 1.6787 × 10⁻¹² for witnesses. This linear identity does not equate the finite-sample direct optimizer with a PSD-based optimizer.

## Assumptions and open questions

The numerical model assumes a stationary permutation-invariant probe, known local encoding, ideal echo controls and population detection, and a calibrated working point. The current design uses 800 Fibonacci measurement axes and a complete PI frame. Exact states and QFI are used by the simulator and for evaluation after freezing, not as learning inputs.

Independent selection reduces reuse of discovery noise, but the selected empirical witness is not itself a certified CFI lower confidence bound. A resource comparison should account for calibration acquisition, control changes, search computation and the accuracy of the competing response optimizer. The present evidence does not establish a smaller informationally complete frame, fewer calibration shots, or a shadow-exclusive advantage.
